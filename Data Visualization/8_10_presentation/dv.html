Does transparency solve overplotting without changing the message?

1. The Claim & Overplotting

We start with a common assumption: "Transparency solves overplotting without changing the message."

When rendering 3,000 data points with solid dots, Harbor hides Old Town. The overlap creates severe visual clutter.

2. Transparency Slicer

Lowering opacity reveals hidden density. Try adjusting the opacity slider below.

Notice that while density becomes visible, low opacity communicates uncertainty or lower importance (similar to confidence intervals).

Desaturation removes color intensity while keeping marks fully opaque. Adjust the saturation slider below.

4. Pre-attentive Attention & "Start with Gray"

Instead of assigning bright colors everywhere, start with a neutral gray baseline.

Use intense color selectively to trigger pre-attentive processing—directing focal attention immediately to critical readings above the limit.

5. Application & Limitations

By highlighting only critical points in red against a gray baseline, the visual story is clear and immediate.

Limitations: Too many focal points cause visual clutter, and color choices must account for colorblind accessibility.
